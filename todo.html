ATell the UIA Ombudsman there are two employees, not one On the same call about owner-wages. A non-owner employee is unambiguously covered for unemployment even if your own wages are not — which may settle the open question in the packet on the spot, and it changes what you register for. Same phone call · 1-855-484-2636
BSet the wage in the defensible range 15 hours a week at $22 is $17,160 a year — ordinary part-time admin pay, and just under the standard deduction so he owes no federal income tax. 20 hours at $18 is $18,720. Anything in that band is easy to justify. Pick a rate and hours
CKeep a timesheet and a one-paragraph job description This is the whole defense. The work has to be real and the pay reasonable for it. With a timesheet it is an ordinary part-time hire; without one it is a disallowed deduction plus penalties. From day one

EBudget it at $1,638 a month, not $1,500 Employer Social Security, Medicare, FUTA and Michigan unemployment add about $1,662 a year on top of the wage. The plan understates the line by $138 a month. Budget correction
FDrop the circular car-insurance transfer The household budget has Eli and Jacob paying $400 a month toward insurance. If one of them is now on payroll you would be paying him so he can pay you back. Cleaner to cover his share and remove the round trip. Tidy-up

What this does not do: there is no family payroll-tax break. The under-18 and under-21 exemptions apply only to sole proprietorships — the S-corp election disqualifies them, and he is 20 regardless. He pays the same 7.65% as anyone. The benefit is that $16,623 of the $18,000 stays in the household and he owes no income tax on it.
