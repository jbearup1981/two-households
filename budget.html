Notes on the figures

Business income
Annualized from $91,401 through 7.5 months — about $146,000 a year. That span includes February and April, the year's two weakest months, and none of the accounts added in July. Deliberately conservative.
Income tax
$16,968 for 2026, married filing jointly — calculated from the published 2026 brackets against actual business profit and the $55,000 W-2 salary, including the QBI deduction. Not a placeholder percentage. It gets withheld from the eight remaining paychecks, so nothing is owed in April.
Health insurance
Settled: Amanda carries all five of us through her employer and maxes the HSA at the $8,750 family limit. That's why there's no premium line in either column — only out-of-pocket.
Actuals vs. estimates
Rent, utilities, the boys' insurance contribution and Amanda's income are real. Groceries, activities, clothing and personal spending are estimates — overwrite them as the actual bills arrive.
IRS back taxes
About $5,000–6,000 owed now plus $5,000–8,000 expected once the 2025 return is filed. That arithmetic reaches $10,000–14,000; it's carried at $20,000 as a safe number. The $6,000 spread is worth resolving — it decides whether the house proceeds clear this line outright.
Why a reserve
2026 ran between $1,425 and $25,038 of net income a month. Two months of seven landed below what it takes to run everything — in a year averaging $12,899. That swing is normal for commission income; having no cash against it is what put $65,000 on the cards. $10,000 in the business covers the deepest month with room; $5,000 in the family account covers a surprise without a phone call.
Car insurance
$750/mo for all four vehicles is a quote, not a bill. Michigan with two teen drivers can come in higher — worth confirming before it's locked in.
