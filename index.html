Payroll & taxes — the part that's been missing

The business became an S-corp on January 1, which means Jason has to be paid a real W-2 salary. That hasn't happened yet this year. Here's the fix, and it's a normal one — the IRS looks at the yearly total, not the timing.

Filing jointly saves us about $8,450 versus filing separately. The whole bill gets withheld from those eight paychecks — no surprise in April, no quarterly payments to remember. Withholding counts as if it were paid evenly all year, which is exactly why doing this now works.

There is still tax owed on the business profit above the salary — that's the $16,968 figure, and it's the number those eight paychecks are sized to cover. Separately, the $500/month going to the IRS is for prior years and continues untouched.
