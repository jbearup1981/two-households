Taxes

The tax situation, in plain English

The business is an S-corp. That's a tax setup, not a different company — same Nexus, filed differently since January 1.

The business itself pays no income tax. Its profit passes through onto our personal return. So there is no such thing as "the business's taxes" separate from ours — it's one bill.

What the S-corp does require is that Jason pay himself an actual paycheck rather than just taking money out. The IRS insists on it. Money he takes beyond that paycheck comes out as a distribution, which skips the 15.3% Social Security and Medicare hit. That split is the entire reason for the S-corp and it saves several thousand dollars a year.

The catch: no payroll has run yet this year. Eight paychecks between September 15 and December 31 fix it — and because the IRS treats withholding as though it were paid evenly across the whole year, catching up now lands us exactly where we'd be had we started in January. No penalty.
